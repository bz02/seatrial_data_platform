Agents fail on the judgment calls nobody wrote down

Models can click through software. What they lack is how your best people decide: which mismatch matters, which exception to escalate, what to check first. That knowledge lives in people's heads and in the work itself. Generic labeled data does not have it, and screen recordings without reasoning do not teach it.

Steps plus reasons

Every action is paired with the expert's spoken or typed reason, so models learn the decision, not just the click.

Verified, not just labeled

Sandbox tasks are graded against a hidden answer key. Real-work captures are checked by model rubric and two-person review.

Every training format

Chat SFT with tool calls, reviewer-corrected DPO pairs, RL environments with verifiers, and eval sets with checkpoints.

Where to collect next

Process discovery maps how work actually flows and shows which workflows have the least training coverage.

How it works

Record

A browser recorder (or our sandbox) logs every action. Experts think aloud by voice or text.

Convert

Events become reasoning → tool call → observation steps, with personal data redacted on the way.

Assess

Answer-key verification, reasoning coverage, near-duplicate detection and model rubric grading. Low scores get two reviewers.

Export

Reviewers fix weak steps; fixes become preference pairs. Export SFT, DPO, RL environments and evals.

Who owns the data in the enterprise edition?

You do. It runs in your environment, recordings stay on your servers, and nothing is used to train anyone else's model.

Do employees know they are being recorded?

Yes. Nothing is recorded without each employee's opt-in, recording only works on apps your admin allows, and employees review and can delete anything before it is submitted.

Is the marketplace data real company data?

No. Expert tasks run on synthetic companies, so there is no client data and no confidentiality problem. The expertise is real; the books are not.

Which models does it work with?

Exports are plain JSONL in common chat and tool-call formats, so they work with any open-weight model or fine-tuning API.
